## Death Drive, the Symbolic, and the Four Discourses

In many of my writings, I have made use of this theorisation, which I called the "washer theory" [théorie de la rondelle]:

![](images/41ae7a5246764b1865290a33ed3632f712c1d0cae694d17c595b935b709f6f1e.jpg)

A discussion on the "psychanalyse" forum about the death drive, with Liliane Fainsilber and Jean‑Baptiste Beaufils, led me to specify the relations between the death drive and the Symbolic, drawing on this quotation from Lacan at the very end of Seminar II:

> "It is here that we arrive at the symbolic order, which is not the libidinal order in which both the ego and all the drives are inscribed. It tends beyond the pleasure principle, beyond the limits of life, and that is why Freud identifies it with the death instinct. You will re‑read the text, and you will see if it seems worthy of your approval. The symbolic order is excluded from the libidinal order, which includes the entire domain of the imaginary, including the structure of the ego. And the death instinct is merely the mask of the symbolic order, inasmuch as — Freud writes it — it is mute, that is, inasmuch as it has not been realised. As long as symbolic recognition has not been established, by definition, the symbolic order is mute. The symbolic order, at once non‑existent and insistent on being, that is what Freud is aiming at when he speaks to us of the death instinct as what is most fundamental — a symbolic order in gestation, in the process of coming into being, insistent on being realised."

To demonstrate this identification made by Lacan between the death drive and the Symbolic, it seemed to me more judicious to present my washer theory as follows:

![](images/03f30af1797cc61ce3ed74c66ef2a991e03ee6b0a4c3a45184eb0c02d9ebe281.jpg)  
In the crowns [couronnes], the signified is the surface, the signifier is the edge.

The constancy of the thrust [poussée] of the death drive: it is the cut [coupure] of the signifier winding around the attractive central hole, the *objet a* cause of desire, represented in hollow by the phallus (the *objet a* is always already lost). As long as it does not cross itself [se recoupe] [^1], it is only the *acoupure* [^2], inasmuch as it cannot detach a piece: it cannot produce an object, which is to say it does not produce a signified. But, crossing itself, it detaches a piece, which we call a signified, with which we construct our "reality", beginning with the image of our body organised around its orifices. The orifices are indeed the places of the relation to the other (a similar other), via the Other (language): the mother interpreting her baby's cry as a demand that a hole be filled (mouth) or emptied (anus), the child's demand that the hole of its eye be filled with her presence, or the mother's demand that it be emptied of it... or inversely, and so on, with all the nuances, inversions and reversals one might wish. For each of these holes, it will be a question for the child, in its relation to the other, of experiencing the *fort‑da* game: putting the Thing outside in order to put a substitute back inside, in the form of a representation. This is the work of the Symbolic and it is the work of the death drive.

And, by crossing itself, the initial *acoupure* becomes the cut [coupure] that produces an object and a hole. Thus is introduced the rhythmics and the discretion (in the sense of discontinuity) of the Symbolic. It is the same cut — it sounds the same, *acoupure* and *coupure* — but they are opposed as the continuous is to the discrete. Like a Möbius strip, which is at once the continuity of the passage from one side to the other, whereby it is only one cut, and the three twists separating three discrete zones — an imposition of writing necessitated by the flattening‑out implied by any relating of a subject and an object (my point of view as a subject on the Möbius‑strip object: there can be no object without a subject to consider it).

In everyday language, the drive [pulsion] is associated with what is animal and savage in man. Hey! Keep your impulses [pulsions] in check!

But we can no longer know anything of what it had at its origin, except by reconstructing it as Freud does, in a mythical way, with *Totem and Taboo*. And even his theory of the drives, he considers a mythology. Indeed, it is made only of *a posteriori* reconstruction, and moreover, when Freud describes its functioning, he notes that it is grammatical: inversion of the subject and the object, reversal onto the subject's own person, transformation of content (love‑hate). From this impossibility of accessing the origin, there remains only a hole, which is the central hole of my writing.

This death drive, fundamentally, is the murder of the Thing: because that is where Freud situated the beginning of humanity, the myth he develops in *Totem and Taboo*. It is from the murder of the dominant male that the murderous brothers are constrained to reach an agreement, and therefore to speak to one another, in order to prevent the return of the tyranny of the strongest. This is the origin of the necessity of the law, notably the one that will henceforth regulate the exchange of women (this time, says Lévi‑Strauss). In short, the law of the incest taboo, which is fundamentally the prohibition of the mother as Thing, that is, as a metaphor for the prohibition of the Thing: words cease to be things. This is attested by the erection of the totem, the phallic symbol of the dead father, guarantor of this law, of this taboo: it is the symbol as such. This is also to return to what Lacan said about the origin of language: it is the first tomb. The father only becomes the father once he is dead, that is, through the effect of the Symbolic, which is to say, of the death drive: before that, he was merely the strongest. It is this murder that then allows the distribution of women, that is, the organisation of the relation to castration. For, to death responds sexuality; the absence of the dead person is metaphorically taken up by the absence of the phallus in the relation between the sexes.

Lacan deduces from this his theory of psychosis, which is in fact not very far from Freud's, at least on this point. Freud said: the psychotic takes words for things. Lacan adds: it is the fault of the foreclosure of the Name‑of‑the‑Father, which is to say that the totem does not function and that the taboo is transgressed.

Finally, the death drive — the one he "discovered" late in his work — what is it? It is the drive for mastery [pulsion d'emprise], in other words the death drive, that is, the attempt of the Symbolic to fabricate representation, wherever a "real" escapes it. The famous curiosity that Freud identifies as always being sexual is precisely this: going to fetch what escapes, what cannot be seen, heard, or understood, in order to bring it back into the pack of representations. What circulates of the real in the drive is rather what the drive excludes, what it circumvents of the real, in attempting to bring it into its circle (see the schema of the drive in Seminar XI); but this always fails: every time something new is represented, catalogued, symbolised, it is to realise that something else, the real, escapes. I represent it *a posteriori* as the yellow surface without limit that serves as the support for the entirety of my writing. Its eviction by the work of the Symbolic is written in the central hole from which a supposed washer called "objet a" has been ejected. A mythical reconstitution of a supposed origin, a reconstitution carried out through language, therefore according to the rules of language, and thus formatted by this language.

This is how Freud develops his examples concerning the theory of the drives: seeking to see, seeking to be seen, seeking to make oneself seen. Beating, being beaten, having oneself beaten. Active, passive, reflexive: this is the functioning of the verb. This is the functioning of the Symbolic. And especially in this drive to beat, to strike, or to have oneself struck, it is precisely the mark of the work of the Symbolic that seeks to make itself master of the other.

Now, it is true that civilisation also imposes limits on this insatiable search. The Symbolic tends, on the one hand, to set limits to the real, and, on the other, to limit itself — what is called setting limits to boundaries. Limits are set on children, and corporal punishment is often justified by this civilising necessity. But that is precisely where adults no longer know their own limits. That is where they present themselves as less civilised than their children. That is where they have not understood that the limits which must indeed be acknowledged must first be imposed on oneself.

The "nature" that one might think is described by the drive is indeed a lost paradigm, as Edgard Morin said. The drive is already civilisation, it is already the Symbolic, it is language. Libido is already an ordering: there is the libido of the ego and the libido of the object, and one is limited by the other. If I am all narcissistic (libido of the ego), I will languish in solitude: I will therefore be pushed to go toward others. If I am too preoccupied with others (object‑libido), my narcissism will push me to isolate myself from time to time.

That is why, concerning the real, the best definition remains the one Lacan gave: it is the impossible; it is not a naturalist definition, it gives no content to the real, and it saves us from a mythical notion such as that of energy. It confines itself to describing the effects that we can observe: each time I encounter an impossibility, well, I call that the real. I cannot go through this wall. I cannot fly. I cannot just speak Chinese straight away. The real resists the hold of the Symbolic, and thus constantly impels the Symbolic to set itself to work. But it is precisely because the Symbolic has created the real, insofar as it is what has escaped the Symbolic; without the Symbolic, there is no real.

In arithmetic, rules have been discovered that make it impossible to do otherwise: here is a real of the Symbolic, but insofar as it excludes all other paths than those demonstrated by the theorems. It is these other paths that are the real.

For example, it is impossible to find a harmonic division of the circumference by the diameter of a circle. This is therefore a real, but a real that can be called "of the Symbolic", because it is an internal limitation to a set of letters posited by the Symbolic; we get out of it by a symbolic *coup de force* that consists in positing the letter π. This is a nice metaphor for analysis, moreover: instead of repeating to infinity the division of the remainder (that is repetition), we posit a letter, like, "I still wet the bed, but I don't care!"

Which ultimately becomes:

![](images/c3a5623f72551469f21715938fa03ad0d946382d3fee69af0fbbc5f61d15ad3d.jpg)

We notice that the central hole, hollowed out of the *objet a*, joins the external hole of the structure. This central hole represents the passage to the phallic function of speech, denoted Φ. Why phallic? Because representation by the signifier — which is audible — is to put something else, something completely ephemeral, in the place of the Thing, which is not there. Now the phallus is this concept of representation, insofar as it puts something masculine in that place where the feminine presents only an absence. In other words, the phallus represents castration, the loss of an organ that gives an image of the loss of the Thing that any act of speech presupposes. This does not happen by itself: this painful loss is marked by a symptom, a letter coming in substitution for what cannot be said.

Speech, in relation to the symptom that does not cease to be written, represents the interpretation that allows it to cease to be written. Speech as such, in the resonance of enunciation, indeed does not get written. What is inscribed is the memory of what one has heard. And it is this — the signifier — that is therefore an edge, with on one side a "hole": the enunciation in act, and on the other a surface: the statement [énoncé], what one retains of what has been said. The cut that surrounds this hole, that is, the signifier representing the *objet a* in discourse, is no longer the *objet a* but a way of speaking about it, a way of speaking about the incompleteness of the Symbolic that castration represents elsewhere.

(For a return to the theory of the four discourses, see: "A Theory of the Borromean Knot" and "Pain" [la douleur].)

It is logical that this internal cut should join the external cut, traversing the entire structure. Each discourse is therefore indeed the minimal articulation of two signifiers, but we must also add the production: this passage from one signifier to another that counts for nothing, and in which one can situate the cause of desire — the cut that the *objet a* occasions between the signifiers. We must also add truth, in the hole hollowed out by the preceding cut.

Starting from the centre, we start from the discourse of the analyst, and this presentation is therefore a *pass* [^3], based on the knowledge of the unconscious brought to light by analysis: it is thus a reconstruction working backwards from the work of the cure. Psychoanalysis is indeed a *disastrology* [^4], since this reverse pearl was a disaster... a descent of ideal stars toward an earth where destinies cease to be fixed by the stars.

I have also called "U" — the discourse of the university — this knowledge issuing from the cure, but it is "P" as in *pass* that it must be named, in accordance with Schema R, which posits "P", the Name‑of‑the‑Father, under A, the big Other — here, the discourse of the analyst.

The continuity between internal and external edge forces us to consider this reconstitution as a Möbius strip with five twists:

![](images/16e97b0aaef5849f8a0ac14c337b56b401e09c1ffcbc25437773c82b71b0f3f6.jpg)

This reconstitution as a Möbius strip conforms to the concept that makes internal and external edges join. Had I chosen conformity to what the surfaces of the preceding drawings offer to the eye, I would have produced a two‑sided [bilatère] strip with four twists. But that would not account for the incompleteness of the discourses that is unveiled at the end of analysis. This incompleteness is constituted as a remainder of this Möbius strip that pushes the cut to continue. It has found its place in discourse, in the form of the assumption of death and castration.

We will also note that only the discourse of the master and the discourse of the hysteric, insofar as they answer each other, are, in their proximity, two‑sided [bilatères] — one above, the other below — while the others remain ambiguous, being both above and below.

We could just as well take up the schema again by reversing the direction of the arrows. That would perhaps be more in line with the direction of analysis, after all:

![](images/b6240c799c45dfe786094c0cf886ded42fcffdf65c41abf1c1e69704d863827d.jpg)

But in arriving at the point where the cut crosses itself [again]? It must necessarily start again towards the outside in order to ratify the perpetuity of discourse. This is a variant of the Möbius strip, a Möbius strip where, upon reaching the inside, one finds oneself on the outside. Taking the algorithm in the first direction, as previously, the logic of the figure imposed on us to write the signifier representing *a* both on the outside and on the inside, if we wanted each discourse to be properly framed by the agent and truth. The *objet a* is therefore indeed *extimate* [^5], as Lacan affirms. Taking the algorithm in reverse, as here, it is the knowledge S2 that we are forced to write both inside and outside. And it is in the logic of the concept that knowledge about objects is none other than the representative of the *objet a*, as an articulation of signifiers producing signifieds. Thus unconscious knowledge articulates itself with conscious knowledge by passing from the inside to the outside, which is none other than the *pass*. This is where the unconscious knowledge brought to light by analysis can, at one's choice, be transformed into university discourse or into the discourse of the pass. What is the pass here? It is the traversal of the discourses once the re‑crossing [recoupe] that allows the detachment of the washer of the *objet a* has been reached. In other words, contrary to the initial approach of this algorithm, where one started from the hypothesis of this primordial detachment, here we cut out crowns of discourse that only truly become crowns at the moment when, the last one crossing itself, it allows the re‑crossing of the other three, by means of the liberation of a washer that is no longer a crown. This also allows us to consider this algorithm as a fractal.

The logic of concepts thus encounters the logic of our figure: there is a necessity to make this algorithm something other than a spiral, which would represent only the infinite slippage of a schizophrenic discourse, by making the *acoupure* cross itself to return to its point of departure. It is also a necessity because otherwise, if it were a simple spiral, that would mean that no discourse, no signified could close back on itself [se boucler]. This referral of the *acoupure* to the outside must traverse its own trajectory 4 times so as to actualise itself as a true cut — *the* cut — in the 4 cases.

The *acoupure* is none other than the trajectory of the signifier, which is also that of castration (Φ), because the word separates from the thing. But the signifier truly takes on its status as a signifier only by articulating itself to another signifier, that is, each discourse articulates with the next. This place where the *acoupure* applies to itself can be named P, from the Name‑of‑the‑Father.

$$
\begin{array}{c} \underline {{(\text { Agent })}} \quad \text { S2 } \quad \xrightarrow {\underline {{\text { U }}} \quad} \underline {{a}} \quad (\text { other }) \\ (\text { truth }) \quad \text { S1 } \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \quad \text {(production)} \end{array}
$$

Like the signifier as such, each discourse is incomplete: its movement is that of the agent over the other (taking into account the local components of the Möbius strip: it locally has two edges), producing the fall of an object, a signified, at the bottom right. Like all signifieds, it is known only by the signifier, which is merely the global taking‑into‑account of the edge of the Möbius strip, combining the two local edges. The "signifier" occupying the place of truth, at the bottom left, remains a potential void that will only be actualised in the following discourse. Each crown is therefore the conceptual equivalent of a Möbius strip with three twists:

### Footnotes

[^1]: *Se recouper* (literally "to cut each other again") here denotes the act of the cut intersecting or closing on itself, thereby transforming a continuous trajectory into a discrete, circular detachment. The English "cross itself" attempts to capture this re‑intersection.

[^2]: **Acoupure** is a neologism formed by analogy with *coupure* (cut), likely with a privative prefix *a‑* or simply as a homophonic variant. In this text it designates the *continuous*, pre‑detachment course of the signifier that has not yet closed in on itself to produce a separate object (signified). It is opposed to *coupure* as the continuous is to the discrete.

[^3]: **Pass** (*la passe*) refers to the institutional procedure in the Lacanian School (École de la Cause freudienne) whereby an analysand testifies to the end of their analysis and is accredited as an Analyst of the School (AE). The author plays on this term to indicate the traversal of discourses.

[^4]: **Disastrology** (*désastrologie*) is a deliberate portmanteau of *désastre* (disaster) and *astrologie* (astrology). It evokes a fall from a celestial/deterministic order toward a terrestrial, contingent truth, marking the deconstruction of ideal destinies in analysis.

[^5]: **Extimate** (*extime*) is Lacan's neologism combining "exterior" and "intimate", designating the *objet a* as both radically foreign and deeply internal to the subject.

[^6]: The term **bilatère** (two‑sided) refers to an orientable surface with two distinct sides, as opposed to the one‑sidedness of a Möbius strip. The author uses it to contrast the master/hysteric discourses with the others.
