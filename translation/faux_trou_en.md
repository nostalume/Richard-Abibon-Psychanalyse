# The false hole is not verified as Real

All of this supports my interpretation of Lacan's work around the false hole[^1]. I have already indicated how the mere position of a judgment, whether true or false, presupposes a proposition – hence a perfectly symbolised imaginary representation – which does not allow the use of the term "Real" as defined by "that which resists symbolisation". The Real thus defined presupposes the foreclosure of judgment, which is not the case when one judges the figure to be false. Concerning the infinite straight line (or the third ring) that comes to hold the false hole in place, Lacan states that it comes "to verify that the hole is real". This is nonetheless a problem, when one recalls that he had just defined the Symbolic as hole[^2]. By thus apposing the true and the real, he shifts the meaning of the word toward reality, in contradiction with what he said on 09/12/75, where he on the contrary opposes truth and the Real: "there is no truth – as such – possible except by hollowing out this Real."

We can go a little further. What are these two rings in need of "verification"? The Symbolic (or sometimes the symbol), says Lacan, and the sinthome. The Symbolic once again loses its definition of "hole" to become a consistency, which itself was defined as Imaginary at the beginning of this seminar. As for the sinthome – formerly symptom – we have known since Freud that it symbolises a conflict between two contradictory representations, or possibly it inscribes a Real trace. It can therefore also represent the work of the Symbolic attacking a piece of the Real encrusted somewhere in the body – unless it is precisely this work that provokes that encrustation.

[^1]: Lacan's "false hole" (*faux-trou*) refers to a topological figure where a hole is simulated or represented, but does not function structurally as a true hole in the symbolic order.
[^2]: In the session of 09/12/75, Lacan stated: "...that it is the hole that I make the essential of what the Symbolic is..."

We have seen, through the exploration of dreams, how the phallus looms on the edge of the Real as one of the representations of the representing machine, when that machine finds itself thwarted. In the allegory of the false hole, the infinite straight line indeed completes the device so that it holds together. One could therefore say that the preceding configuration, with two rings (above), was a theoretical representation of the hole that does not hold – therefore Real – in contradiction with my first interpretation, which made this writing a Symbolic, solely by virtue of the fact that it is a writing. The phallus, in the form of the infinite straight line, comes to this place not to verify, but to transform the configuration into a representation that holds... in reality, by verifying the Imaginary of the sexual relation. After the event *[après‑coup]*, we can still affirm that this presentation of the hole that did not hold corresponds to the absence of representation of the feminine in the unconscious. The phallus represents it as an effect of castration: where there should be, there is not. Such are the untenable terms of the contradiction. We find traces of this in my double interpretation of what is always a writing, even if one pretends to abstract it in order to believe in the reality of the string rings it represents.

We could also say: these two rings coupled two contradictory representations – this symbol making symptom, or the contrary if one prefers (the symptom making symbol). But the phallus always comes to represent what makes them symbolise each other, which elevates the symptom to its symbolic function. It also makes a symbol of what comes to symbolise the work of the Symbolic as thwarted by the Real. Indeed, the figure pierced by the infinite straight line is a new writing which, in order to hold as a representation, is not for that reason a representation of the previous one, but its complete transformation.

![](images/477e0ebeaa79ffc37bffcba1c1a9301040290b9bf0812d14d9a84d3024959ca5.jpg)

These two interpretations seem to me consistent with the practice of dream and symptom analysis. But in all cases, one cannot say that the hole obtained is Real: it is a symbolic writing imagining the hole, verified as true. It is in the *après‑coup* of this knotting that one can say something about the two rings: for example, that the symptom was the symbol of two contradictory representations, or that the preceding writing theoretically wrote the practical impossibility of writing the feminine in the psyche. But the Symbolic – keeping to the chosen definition of "hole" – is not the symbol, which here is a consistency, i.e., a ring of string, i.e., an Imaginary. The Symbolic is the hole once it has been verified by the phallic straight line – verified as true and not as Real.

In any case, from a purely semantic clarity point of view, if we have chosen to define the Symbolic as hole, then no hole can be Real.
