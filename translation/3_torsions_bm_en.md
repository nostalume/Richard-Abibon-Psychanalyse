# Richard Abibon

## The Three Twists of the Möbius Strip

> "That which is said remains forgotten behind what is said in what is heard" (Lacan, *L'Étourdit*).

I paraphrase: that one must *construct* the Möbius strip in order to be able to hold it in one's hand is forgotten by those who say something about it, in what is "well understood" both in the mathematical field and in the psychoanalytic field.

---

## Introduction: a clinical example of the usefulness of the topological tool

I had begun by treating this question in a purely topological manner. Then I said to myself that, on the one hand, no one would understand anything, and that, on the other hand, it is important not to lose oneself in endless mathematical calculations, especially calculations that have no relation to our preoccupation: accounting for psychoanalysis as a practice. Otherwise, what would theory be for?

To introduce the question of the three twists of the Möbius strip simply, I would say that it refers to the local/global difference. A word, locally in a sentence, engenders such and such a signified. But replaced in its global context, it engenders another signification, which was latent as long as one did not make it manifest by reference to the global context. Polemics about quotations are made of nothing but that.

But if it were only a matter of polemics between intellectuals, it wouldn't be serious. So I go directly to an example.

One of my analysands tells me this: "When I was little, I killed another little boy with a blow from a stick; then a few months later I blinded another boy by tipping a pot of paint over his head." Local quotation of his speech, green zone.

![](images/514b3836805655e26df0d859eb6bc325162221e57d3634064bbdcafef3b5d24b.jpg)

Later, as he often comes back to this, I ask him: "How do you know that this boy died? Did you see him again? And this boy who was blinded, did you see him? Where do you get that from?" – "It was my father who told me." Global quotation of the speech: we only had one zone of the Möbius strip, green, and here we are given back the other side, red, which allows us to understand retroactively that the green and red zones (the speech in the form of words taken for things) were in fact condensed into a single yellow side, which was taken for green. We have passed from a statement as a thing to its restitution in its context: this statement was the fact of an enunciation by my analysand's father, who took it up in his own enunciation, forgetting this shift of the subject of enunciation.

This results, for this analysand, in the following global trajectory: "If I really killed, well, I've paid enough, I've punished myself enough; and if I didn't kill, there's no reason for me to punish myself; in either case I have every reason to stop punishing myself. But more probably, this story of killing and blinding was my delusions."

In passing, he brushes against the concept of the signifier of a lack in the Other (S(A): S of barred A): there is no guarantor of truth, he says in substance. The trajectory of the three twists of the Möbius strip leads him to realise that the whole strip turns around a void – this void that precisely allows the twists, just as the void around a coin allows one to pass from heads to tails. This is truly considering the object no longer from the point of view of the object but from the point of view of the subject who has an object and who takes himself into account in the elaboration of his point of view on the object. Moreover, in this case, the object is himself.

But to take up this trajectory more concretely:

**Green side**: words are presented as things; there is an indisputable fact: I killed and I blinded.

**Red side**: but the opposite can be true: I neither killed nor blinded.

**Yellow side**: it was my father who told me. The accent shifts from the surface to the hole, from the statement to the enunciation. From the said to the saying.

A 2nd turn is inaugurated under the auspices of the cut: and saw... and saw...:

**Green side**: "If I really killed, well, I've paid enough, I've punished myself enough..."

**Red side**: "and if I didn't kill, there's no reason for me to punish myself";

**Yellow side**: "...in either case I have every reason to stop punishing myself. But more probably, this story of killing and blinding was my delusions."

In other words, the accent is placed on the fact of saying, not on the said. It is no longer an indisputable fact; it is the interest of the discussion as such – i.e., as exchange, passage, relation of one subject to another (and no longer only to an Other). The passage from the 1st to the 2nd turn occurred through the displacement of the accent onto a subject of enunciation: "it was my father who said it"; the 2nd turn is entirely the putting into act of a new subject of enunciation – himself – by the very fact that the discourse regains its hypothetical character. This is the installation of the third time of the drive, the one where Freud says that it is there that "a new subject" appears ("Instincts and their Vicissitudes", 1915). In Seminar XI, Lacan emphasises that it is there that the subject appears, quite simply.

There are therefore two readings of the yellow side:

**Object**: it condenses two contradictory propositions; it represents the function frozen as object.

**Function**: it represents the movement of passage from one proposition to the other – i.e., the twist as such, the totality of the Möbius strip as such, which is a triple twist, when the function, instead of being blocked as object, begins to function again as function – i.e., when a subject speaks, at the moment he speaks.

The yellow zone is both above and below. In the first acceptation, it remains a disoriented surface, both above and below – a disoriented object. In the second, it is a displacement of investment from the surface to the edge; the edge itself being also a twist – i.e., a passage from above to below, considered as movement, as function. It therefore writes the edge as a surface (1st time: passage from 1 dimension to 2 dimensions), then the surface as an edge (2nd time: passage from 2 dimensions to 1 dimension): it is the totality of the edge of the Möbius strip that is concerned – the totality that is both above and below: this is the signifier, as opposed to the signified and signification (surfaces).

This allows us to rethink the whole trajectory theoretically.

In the first enunciation: "I killed... etc...", the speech appears as a fact, a thing. There is no evocation of a contrary speech, nor any nuance in terms of hypothesis. There is no mention of the subject of enunciation, who finds himself bogged down in his statement, which will later turn out to be that of another. It is therefore a surface that presents itself as a signified, green, but it has no reverse. If it has no reverse, it is because it is front-back everywhere. Therefore the edge is not differentiated from the surface: the edge – i.e., the signifier, the one that refers to the subject of enunciation – is not distinct from the surface, which is the statement. The local of the yellow surface is in fact global; there is no local/global difference, and we are therefore constrained to write this on a homogeneous Möbius strip[^1], characteristic of psychotic discourse:

![](images/3bc6cce47fd5cb9b89a5ab124df13337527352fdd7ced120c578425ad2d93d01.jpg)

The continuation of the analysis allows the reintroduction of difference – i.e., of the heterogeneous. One can say that it is the naming of the subject of enunciation that effected this cut: "It was my father who said it", which restores the Name-of-the-Father in its function. The function functions in the sense that it produces the heterogeneous – the analysable (orientable-red), the analysed (oriented-green), and an unanalysable remainder (unorientable-yellow):

![](images/ed1bca7d2e2538ee7722f5e1b5aea1289ab62d79ef16e2746655734ba17a9c23.jpg)

These are the "holes in the hole-making" – i.e., the frozen functions P₀ and Φ₀ of Schema I (in the *Écrits*: "On a Question Preliminary to Any Possible Treatment of Psychosis") – which are restored to their functioning state as P and Φ of Schema R.

## Lacan and the Möbius strip: last words

After having abandoned the Möbius strip for a time to occupy himself with the Borromean knot, Lacan returns to it at length in his last two seminars, *The Moment to Conclude* (77/78) and *Topology and Time* (78/79).

On 11 April 78, Lacan introduces the Möbius strip which he calls "double" (this is the one he will later call "triple", which I will call "homogeneous" in what follows). It does not come just anyhow, not as a collage or an illustration, but as a "symbolisation" of sexuality:

> "There is no sexual relation, except for neighbouring generations – namely the parents on one side, the children on the other. That is what the prohibition of incest responds to – I speak of the sexual relation. Knowledge is always in relation to what I write as 'the asex' on condition that it is followed by the word to be put in parentheses, 'uality': asex(uality). Knowledge 'as hell' – that is at least how I write it. I began at one time to make, in order to symbolise this sexuality, a Möbius strip. I would now like to correct this strip, I mean by that, to triple it."

Lacan then produces the following two drawings:

![](images/bd8375eb264843079da211fa96a6f945a8878cc0cb877b5db01d4a161eeb570e.jpg)

...continuing:

> "This is a strip just like the other – namely that its front coincides with its back, but this time it happens twice. (...) it is therefore a double Möbius strip."

In so doing, he compares the carp and the rabbit. He compares in fact two modes of writing, and finds differences where there are none, not seeing the differences that two comparable modes of writing would bring out. It is even worse than that, since the figure on the left is a carp-rabbit chimera: if the fold we read in its upper part is a flattening, the loop to be read in its lower part is a bizarre thing that cannot be assimilated to a flattening. As for the figure on the right, the writings it contains contradict what it itself writes: in what it writes itself, the upper fold separates two different sides, whereas their labels are identical. The label of the lower zone is the only one that is different, which would suggest that the zones on the right and left are the same zone – but this is not the case, since the upper twist divides them. For the strip that Lacan writes on the left in his beautiful curves, if one restores it in a complete flattening, can also be written as follows[^2]:

![](images/d0c454a6a7ecfda096c25f15722bfee3d671ed742bbbba07dbafcd49610875c4.jpg)

The loop has been analysed by flattening: it is composed of two twists. The loop in the previous drawing does not correspond to much, except to say, by default: since this is not a flattening, then it is a representation in space.

... and the whole of Lacan's demonstration on "it passes twice from back to front" falls, because on this writing of the so-called "one-twist" strip, there are also three twists, and it also passes twice from front to back. Moreover, if we read the writing of the strip as such, in the writing of the so-called "three-twist" strip, it is in fact impossible to read a difference between the twists, any more than between the zones they delimit: they are all exactly alike, and therefore, Lacan's labels "front-back" and "back-front" introduce a difference where there is none. On the other hand, the writing above gives us a very clear difference to read, both between the twists and between the three surface zones.

This is a fundamental problem – that of nomination. One could very well take a figure that is only an equilateral triangle, for example, and arbitrarily decide to name its three vertices differently. But then one is not doing topology: one is making schemas and reasoning about nominations, not about the figure, because the three vertices of a triangle are not different from each other. The difference is introduced there by an extrinsic nomination. In my view, doing topology consists in reading to the letter what characterises the figure intrinsically, and naming its characteristics. Then one reasons about the figure, which can thereby take the status of a letter in the Lacanian sense of the term, or in the Freudian sense of thing-representation. It is a letter because it is read as such. One does not plaster onto it differences that cannot be read there.

On the other hand, one could not give a writing of the Möbius strip totally "in space", because all writing is by definition done on a supporting plane. One could then "say", without writing, that the strip-object in space localises no twist anywhere, and thus that the twist would be everywhere. This would be situating oneself in an idealism of speech that would join the objectivism of the object in an in-difference far removed from analysis. This would be supposing that there exists a speech without writing – i.e., a speech without memory, an enunciation without a psychic apparatus, a conscious without an unconscious, an enunciation without repression, an analysis without resistance, and finally: an object without a subject to grasp it... for every point of view on the object functions as a projection of that object onto a plane.

The difference that Lacan does not name between the two strips – the so-called "one-twist" and the so-called "three-twist" – lies in the direction of one of the twists. This can be read by placing side by side the two comparable writings:

![](images/a2d5176eb71e6eb0fe17d478f6bae0278d47ed0d47c4db3c75672d902f54408e.jpg)

![](images/96c38166f24f651cf8a57b5690b8faa44933263de3b4b065eb143c1f861b28a6.jpg)

Nevertheless, in the immediate continuation of his remarks, it seems to me that he draws the correct consequences from this so-called "doubling". Despite a beginning where it is not very clear whether he is speaking of the one on the left or the one on the right, it appears to me, from what he sets out, that he is indeed speaking of the one on the right, and in my view, this is to indicate what happens in incest:

> "But the distinction between this (on the right) and that (on the left) lies in the fact that it is possible to have a Möbius strip which, on its two sides, is both front and back. There is a single side on each side: it is a Möbius strip that has the property of being bilateral. What do we lose in abstraction? We lose the tissue, we lose the fabric – that is to say, we lose what presents itself as a metaphor. Likewise, I point out to you, art – the art by which one weaves – art is also a metaphor."

For all this discussion has meaning for us analysts only in relation to psychoanalysis and, as Lacan recalls well in his introduction, to sexuality. The thread of his discourse confirms this in what follows: he speaks to us of "lalangue", of free association, of the dream, of the Oedipus complex, and of this thread that weaves metaphors. Incest, since Oedipus is in question, undoes the weaving that conferred a surface on the strip I call "heterogeneous" (on the left), and leaves only the thread of the "homogeneous" strip (on the right). It is the opposite-direction twist that allowed a stopping point when one pulls on the thread. This stop – this quilting point – disappears when the differences in direction disappear: sense itself fades away, so true is it that it is born of the difference in direction. Only the thread of free association remains, which can give the impression of deliring:

> "Freud, in *The Interpretation of Dreams*, does no better: on the dream, by free association, on the dream, he dreams... how to know where to stop in the interpretation of dreams? It is quite impossible to understand what Freud meant in *The Interpretation of Dreams*. This is what made me delire – one must say it – when I introduced linguistics into what is called an effective paste, at least we suppose so, and which is analysis."

Here, Lacan joins Freud when he speaks of the schizophrenic impression of the analyst's discourse (I quote *in extenso* this passage from the "Metapsychological Supplement to the Theory of Dreams", at the end of this work). He realises that the flattening of the Möbius strip "is nothing other than a three-thread" – i.e., a trefoil. In passing, he notes that "flattening something always serves a purpose". Indeed, he might have thought to complete the flattening of the other Möbius strip – the heterogeneous one – by flattening its "loop" part, left for some unknown reason "in space". He might have realised what precisely prevents the thread from unravelling and the fabric of metaphor from preserving itself in its dynamic. On this point, see below the chapter I devote to "a writing of psychosis".

Flattening gives itself to be read as writing, and this is an analysis; this is what happens when a subject lies flat on the couch: he realises that what he carries within him in the way of writings as surface resists his speech, because, in analysing himself, in flattening himself in his own writings, he plunges into them. He can read not only the traces, but also the erasures of the trace – themselves writings of the plunging as such, loss of the third dimension, representation of representance.

![](images/45e7892cae04e301f0ab829b21b631cd5366eef1629bd9310ea64714f2b5cfb0.jpg)

From these two dimensions of the writing plane, as soon as he has grasped the mechanism of plunging (repression), he can straighten himself up and put into "I" in the void of space – the void of this absence of trace that will make new speech.

In his difficulty in saying what it is about, Lacan holds a discordantial discourse for us: he says in one unknown stroke: "There is a single side on each side: it is a Möbius strip that has the property of being bilateral." If he only wanted to tell us that there is only one side, he would have said "unilateral". But he tells us: "a single side on each side." He is visibly looking for a vocabulary, and the continuation of his associations indicates to us what he is circling around. It is truly a moment when he is an analysand, stumbling on a Real and circling around it with what, of the graspable, comes to his mind. And there, we see the concepts of psychoanalysis coming to the rescue of topology; but the concepts as he, Jacques Lacan, introduced them, handled them, kneaded them – the way in which he himself plunged into the "effective paste" of psychoanalysis.

## He seeks, but he does not find

In my view, the thread he seeks is that of the trefoil, which is that of the homogeneous Möbius strip such that, being unilateral on each of its three sides, it loses its fabric consistency. If a side is both above and below, what is it? It is an edge: it thereby loses its materiality as fabric. It is no longer this weaving, or knitting (to wink at René Lew), which shuttles between one edge and the other; it is no more than thread. Now, here, in the homo strip, all sides are both above and below. This homo strip is no more than thread. It represents the passage onto the edge – the passage to word-representations alone, somewhat in the manner of analytic interpretation. The latter is however not the same thing as schizophrenia. The former is represented by the edge of the hetero strip, the latter by the edge of the homo strip (see below "A Writing of Psychosis").

The hetero strip in writing – in its flattening – is unilateral only on one of its three sides. In Lacan's mouth, as in Terrasson's, the word "materiality" often returns in this session of the seminar, to indicate what holds the edges together. He would have been happier to speak of surface as the product of two dimensions (metaphor and metonymy: nothing to do with any matter whatsoever), or of fabric as the product of the shuttling movement – the knitting of the two edges. The other sides are bi: the two other sides are one the reverse of the other. Thus the surface maintains a gap between the edges – a gap that frays in the homogeneous strip... provided one reads the concept written by this homo-hetero difference, without being fooled by the image each of them draws.

In other words, on the hetero strip, the function – the edge – engenders the object – the bilateral surface, with two sides. This is reversible: the unilateral object, between the two edges, guarantees the gap between the two sides of the bilateral object. One could just as well say that it causes it; it ensures that the function functions by producing an object distinct from the function.

The function functions in the twist that makes one pass from One side to the Other. But it is represented as object by the unilateral side opposed to it.

On 18 April 79, Lacan returns to this comparison of the two strips, elevating the "three-twist" strip to the title of "the true Möbius strip", as opposed to the one that presides over the cover of *Scilicet*, which is supposed to have a single twist. Yet on the cover of *Scilicet*, the three twists are perfectly readable. It is a flattening such as he had called for it the previous week.

On this occasion, Lacan inaugurates the vocabulary suggested to him by Jean-Claude Terrasson: he speaks of "half-twist". I find that this formula unnecessarily weighs down the vocabulary, especially since it does not appear justified to me. "Half-twist" refers to the fact that, to change sides, one must accomplish a half-turn – i.e., 180°. In that case, a complete twist would be to return to the starting side. Now, returning to the starting side is an operation that allows some topologists to say that the two half-twists cancel each other out, which would entail their erasure from the writing, as in the drawing I incriminated above as incorrect. The paradox of the appellation "half-twist" is that, then, the complete twist turns out to be equivalent to a null operation for those very people who use this vocabulary.

I therefore prefer to call "twist" the operation that consists in passing from one side to the other; it is clearer and less heavy, especially since Lacan and many others regularly slip into calling "twist" what I indeed call a twist.

In this same session of the seminar, Terrasson produces a table of objects with zero, one, two, three twists, cheerfully comparing, he too, what is not comparable – i.e., writings that are not governed by the same principles of flattening.

Thus he indicates the cylinder as an object with zero twists, producing in support a drawing in perspective, and not a flattening. Yet the reading of this writing allows one to identify two twists there:

![](images/6df90c71f00614718272ad7f70f3a95a0fcb5ebd0d3ad66695f1a3bf48b68654.jpg)

This is simply another kind of flattening, since in any case we are on the flatness of the writing support. This other kind, we can call it "perspective" or "drawing", because it puts into play a certain law of writing, which had great difficulty emerging in the history of drawing and painting, but which now is there. We will therefore distinguish this kind of flattening called "perspective" (or drawing) from the other kind of flattening, which we will call "flattening" (or writing) properly speaking.

Terrasson indicates that the two edges of this object, "if one draws them like this:

![](images/f232e8ecee6bd8580f3dd566db4326c10041f2e5f35f77af5835f9f57c0f8790.jpg)

...they are only linked by the fact that the strip has a certain materiality to link these two edges."

The appeal to the materiality of the object allows one to dispense with what is nonetheless essential: that these edges can be read as twists – i.e., passage from one side to another side. These movements of passage occur without our knowledge: our eye successively sees one side then the other in their continuity, and we read, in our inner self, a maxim of this kind: ah, there is an inner side and an outer side, but it is the same object. It is our eye that makes the shuttling movement from one side to the other, knitting "the materiality of fabric" of the surface. It is the same side, because if the twist is not written by a fold, it is written by an edge, and from one side to the Other side, it is nonetheless the same fabric. It would therefore be more accurate to say: it is the same object, and it has two sides woven from the same thread. This is a writing artefact, but one realises that it is impossible to read the object otherwise, even when one looks at it in its three dimensions as an object: one sees the two sides simultaneously, and one deduces that it is indeed the same fabric. Our eye performs, without our knowledge, the twisting movement – i.e., the implicit flattening of the object, its projection onto a plane.

I appealed not to the materiality of the object, but to the reading capacities of the subject.

A certain flattening, when one crushes the cylinder onto a plane, would reveal a simple square or rectangle, as Terrasson himself points out. However this flattening would not yet be a writing because it would not allow the hole to be read – the hole that nonetheless separates the two sides; one could not distinguish it from the drawing of a simple square. For flattening to be writing, it must be an analysis – i.e., it must account for all the elements involved, including the holes. This is roughly what the drawing with the two twists does. But a true writing – a flattening obtained from a strip presenting sufficient difference between width and length – leads one to read 4 twists, in opposite directions two by two:

Three writings of the same object: the cylinder

One reads there that a cylinder is in fact the continuity of two loops – one read "front" and the other "back" – the loop being what is written as a loop in the drawing of the so-called "one-twist" Möbius strip[^5].

Let us return to Terrasson's catalogue. Right next to it, as an object with one twist, we see, contrary to the cylinder in perspective, a complete flattening of the Möbius strip where, as on the cover of *Scilicet*, the three twists are perfectly readable!

The object presented as having two twists is a strange hybrid: the two twists are flattenings, and what connects them appears as a loop – the same loop that closes the twist in the "one"-twist object, of which we have seen that flattening always reveals two twists. The so-called "two-twist" object in fact has four twists. It differs from the cylinder only by a different succession in the direction of the twists.

Finally, the three-twist object is presented in a writing that is exactly that of the one-twist object (i.e., three!), except for the direction of one of the twists, which no one mentions. In the drawings at my disposal, included in Lacan's seminar, one senses that Terrasson's so-called "one-twist" Möbius strip is an attempt at representation "in space": the corners are rounded, as if they wanted to be read in a denegative manner: "no, this is not a flattening!" Nevertheless, they are there, and they indeed draw three twists, exactly as in the drawing of the so-called "three-twist" strip; which demonstrates the impossibility of a representation in space... since we are still and always in the plane.

These remarks allow us to note here a homology of the three-twist Möbius strip with the Borromean knot. When one glues two ends of a "zero-twist" strip, each of the future folds you make does indeed pass from one side to the other (locally, and from your point of view). But since one does it twice, this function (passing from one side to the other) cancels itself out, and it makes a ring. One is still on two separated sides (globally). It is the addition of a third twist that makes two twists not cancel each other out, as regards the function of passing from one side to the other. This is a remark to be compared with: there is no Borromean knot with two rings. A third is needed for the three to hold. It is indeed a matter of three rings as it is of three twists. This is why, from the conceptual point of view (and not from the stupid point of view of the object, of course), the hetero strip is homologous to the Borromean knot, while the homo strip is analogous to the trefoil.

On 21 November 78 – first session of *Topology and Time* – Lacan returns again to this comparison of the two strips. He performs a numbering of the 3 sides of the flattening of each, and observes that this gives an identical reading on both. He deduces from this that "the enveloping strip has two edges", which the demonstration I give below confirms, by specifying the conceptual nature of these edges: it is the signifier. This is true for both strips.

In which case, one must read between the lines here, and read that Lacan speaks of the writing of the Möbius strip, for the Möbius strip as object has only one edge, of course.

On 19 December 78, he confirms this reading by means of the torus: "A torus, for example, is a surface without edge. Nevertheless a torus can be flattened, and if one flattens it, it makes a surface with edges. It is even for that reason that the torus can serve to make a Möbius strip. This is how it is drawn. It makes a Möbius strip on condition of flattening it. But one can reinflate it, in which case it becomes a torus again. It remains nonetheless that the torus and the Möbius strip are distinct..."

This recurrence of the question of flattening is crucial for psychoanalysis: flattening is writing – i.e., the formalisation of what cannot be said, what cannot be grasped, and which is written in the formations of the unconscious. Flattening produces edges where there were none: this is an adequate metaphor for psychoanalysis, by which the flattening on the couch, but above all, the taking of speech, engenders word-representations (the edges) where there were only thing-representations (surface) in the unconscious.

I will therefore provide a few demonstrations in support.

## Arguments for the single-twist Möbius strip

The arguments for the single-twist Möbius strip are as follows: in the Möbius strip object, the single twist is everywhere. The other two are only a flattening artefact. Moreover, the two opposite-direction twists, + and –, on either side of the "One" surface, are supposed to cancel each other out: since in "–" one has passed from one side to the other, and in "+" one has returned to the previous side, there is no reason to take them into account. This is reasoning as in arithmetic: 1 – 1 = 0. But if one reads this formula correctly, 1 – 1 is indeed still there, written in the left part of the equals sign. To consider it as nothing is to perform that operation of erasure of a representation that Freud calls repression. On the contrary, not erasing this left part is to do analysis. The essence of the Freudian discovery lies there: we all have contradictory drives which, for the conscious, cancel each other out... and nonetheless continue their work in the unconscious, which does not know contradiction.

These arguments for the single twist take the point of view of the object, not that of writing. In fact, they imply an implicit writing, for every theory is writing – a point of view on the object, a construction of the object. Now, there can be no object in itself. Now, this implicit theory is situated on the side of the sides of the object as a point of reference – i.e., from the point of view of the surface, and not from that of the movement which engenders this single surface in a triple form: globally (i.e., in space – i.e., again, in the representation we have of space... which is a writing... necessarily in a plane!) there is only one side. This theory therefore denies what lies between the sides – whether one calls it the edge, the cut, or movement, the latter being the taking into account of time. For it takes a certain time to accomplish the operation of passing from One side to Another, then to pass back to the One side.

Exactly the same thing happens when Freud tells us to take account of this expression: "it is not my mother". We could consider it as nothing: since the subject said it was not his mother, well, there it is, it is not his mother. Since we have returned to the same side, well, we do not have to take account of the intermediate side, nor of the twist by which we accessed it, nor of the twist by which we left it. Yet, Freud observes, the representation "mother" appeared in the discourse in a negative form, and this is not to be neglected. What is at stake is the operation of representance, which functions fundamentally in the form of the negative. Thus in the *fort-da*, the first movement, and the most repeated, is the *fort* – the movement of throwing away. This is the very movement of the Symbolic, which writes by this gesture a representation of the mother's departure – a representation in the negative form of this movement of rejection.

Similarly, we read in our writing an affirmative twist (+): "mother", and a negative twist (–): "it is not". As analysts, would we say that we are not going to take account of the first twist? It takes a certain time in analysis – a logical time – before the signifier "mother" appears: a second side, in the writing of the cure, after a first where it appears in a negative form. But "repression is not yet lifted," Freud tells us – it is my mother and it is not her: the two sides are there at the same time, in the same sentence, which appears in the frozen totality of a holophrase.

![](images/73aed6562ee6da292da0cdec9b53ec465bc63b1c04b3912fd96ed189b9e52dff.jpg)

Finally, with one more turn on the Möbius strip – an interpretative turn in the cure – the signifier "mother" can be included in the discourse in a positive form, and this is the third side. More precisely, the recognition of the possible positivity of the representation "mother" allows one to accept situating oneself in the "neither... nor" (yes, she is indeed my mother, but she is not her, the one who will have been my object; it is only the reconstruction I have made of her in my analysis: a representation) of a speech freed from the fixity of the object and returned to the mobility of its function.

The surface will have lost its colour, and above all, its capacity to fix movement, to reduce itself to the signifying thread that makes the Möbius strip a pure blue edge.

![](images/897ae3fdcab31ac28b37f0722f065d3085caa09de0779d03efce7c8843ef5a1f.jpg)

Yes, it is indeed my mother, but it is not her – the one who will have been my object; it is only the reconstruction I have made of her in my analysis: a representation, it was only a stand-in for the *objet a*.

![](images/4181765f79d7363d8247a439e7ffb9c953e99008f1ee07517be30efeb175e2c5.jpg)

We arrive at making explicit the implicit theory that is read in a writing of the Möbius strip taken for a single-twist object: this represents a particular enunciation, in what it has of the instantaneous – vanishing as soon as it appears, just like the subject. It is not false, but it does not make explicit this ternarity in which all enunciation is situated: that one signifier (+) represents a subject (–) for another signifier (+).

Thus this writing of the three-twist Möbius strip: yes, it is indeed the Möbius strip, but it is not it – it is only the reconstruction I have made of it in my analysis: a representation that makes its structure explicit, conforming to the structure of the signifier.

## Demonstration 2: manual work

Since when is flattening an artefact? No more than the flattening of the analysand on the couch, which brings about that very useful artefact called transference. Flatten a well-formed Möbius strip onto any plane: does it or does it not have three twists? In what way would this be an artefact? It is an effect of art, certainly, and that's fine: art is human. Art is made. Nothing human that does not pass through language, and this discordantial negation I have just employed indicates how writing supplements the flaw of the Symbolic: what I cannot say, I can write by means of the formations of the unconscious: slip, parapraxis, dream, symptom. Provided that something is said about it *après-coup*: read what these formations write. Reading aloud this writing of the formations of the unconscious always induces transference – the artefact by which the fixation of writing will precisely be able to surpass itself in the movement of speech thus engaged.

So, rather than focusing on the object that blocks representance, let us analyse the movement by which we construct the Möbius strip object.

If we make a twist on a strip of paper, we obtain this:

![](images/c795056af4ad9d8e883dd146f3795eb4d71fed916e5676f6c08a944c9fd5e16c.jpg)

We have indeed made a single twist, but what we have obtained is more a bow tie, not a Möbius strip. It is elegant, but insufficient. To construct a Möbius strip we still need to glue the right edge to the left edge.

![](images/7df09ba6846efcc9eddf5f0e98eaeb441200bf30b82a112c5b6f0e52df7fe66f.jpg)

A movement, usually unnoticed, by which we add a twist on the left (+: from above to below[^6]) and a twist on the right (+: from above to below), both of which oppose the primitive twist (– from below to above). Without these two twists, there is no "single side" to the Möbius strip, since we have not put one edge and the other, one side and the other, into continuity.

Are these not twists? Yet observe the writing above: on each side, the strip now presents its front side and its back side, exactly as in the previous writing, which only mentioned a single twist.

A writing artefact[^7]? But to make these twists – without which there would be no Möbius strip – a subject is needed. This subject, while performing these operations, necessarily develops a point of view. It is this point of view that the above writing represents; would you like to suppress the subject to arrive at a point of view of the pure object "in itself", while continuing elsewhere to denounce ontological discourse?

Of course, the said subject can then go around points of view to arrive at a more global point of view. It would be fortunate if this did not suppress him as subject. This is precisely what I propose by allowing him to analyse the movement by which he constructed his object – his *objet a* – which he can now throw into the wastepaper basket, along with the strip.

All this corresponds to the movement of flattening that the subject performs by lying down on the couch. He lies down in the two dimensions of the fantasy in order better to analyse its lineaments through the voice, which remains in the third dimension: while becoming gradually capable of reading the flattening of the fantasy, it never ceases not to be written.

## Twist: precision in the definition and square Möbius strip

## Specifying Demonstrations 2 and 3 of the three twists of the Möbius strip

On 24 November 2012, while once again presenting the theory of the three twists to the working group composed of Marie‑Laure Caussanel, Michel Thomé, and François Duchenne, I realised, notably thanks to a remark by Marie‑Laure, that one could specify the following: one must better define what a twist is. Until now, I said: it is the function that makes one pass from one side to the other. That is correct. But one can specify: it is the function that reverses the third dimension – the one that is not in the writing page – from +z to –z. Now, this cannot occur without the reversal of the other dimensions that construct the surface, x and y. "The" twist – the one everyone makes when preparing a Möbius strip – consists in reversing the width, i.e., +y to –y. On that, everyone will agree. The gluing – which everyone insists on calling "fold" and not twist – consists in reversing the length twice, i.e., +x to –x. Now, if from an intuitive point of view one differentiates length and width by saying: length is greater than width, what intrinsic difference is there between the two dimensions of any surface? Especially since, in topology, measurement should be indifferent to us. The reversal of the width also reverses depth – i.e., the third dimension, z – bringing out the underside. The reversal of the length reverses depth in the same way, also bringing out the underside. It is exactly the same thing. Consequently, I ask those who insist on calling the two gluing twists "folds", in order to deny that they are twists: what, according to you, is the difference? How do you then define twist, so as to distinguish it from fold?

![](images/b05d67a824ee00df00f07868f6cdc56a6f3ddbf020ce310a428a9d42d7e7b1f8.jpg)  
Twist according to width

Every twist reverses the third dimension, and this is accompanied by the reversal of either one or the other of the two dimensions of the surface.

The following construction of the square Möbius strip supports this remark: there, no more difference of measurement between the dimensions. Moreover, the constraints thus generated make all difference between fold and twist disappear. Here, one feels one is only making folds. They are all identical, and yet they are twists.

## Demonstration 3: manual work – construction of the square Möbius strip

Shortcut meaning: construction of the Möbius strip from a square. You have noticed that what characterises a strip – the most common strip, the one we use to construct Möbius strips – is the inequality of the measurement of these edges. We usually start from a rectangle. Try to perform the three twisting movements indicated above starting from a square! You will find that you seriously lack amplitude for your movement. A metaphor for neurotic blockage in a symptom? Why not... some people who like to be very square, in morality or metaphysics, in logic or mathematics, might suggest this.

It is nonetheless possible, with a little ingenuity:

![](images/271384842037a2744e93f5b5493b065924b7f175f269be68bfa4ce86c69ec24f.jpg)

Fold the square along a diagonal (one twist, +): in the writing (the point of view of the folder), it is the Other side that appears. That makes one twist.

![](images/78dc71f3af455cb304f6172d108018798414dad95f336c12116409667ee6fe13.jpg)

Fold the obtained triangle along the opposite diagonal. It is another portion of the Other side that appears. That makes two twists (another twist, +).

![](images/3c3c07551054598c0e27b75c14df7ecaa1ef6fc6d65e688f1be9a3e9f4281dca.jpg)

If we have taken care to colour One and the Other, we can identify, among the four edges constituting the base of the obtained triangle, those that form the edge of One side, and those that form the edge of the Other side.

They are opposed two by two. To identify one side and the Other side, it suffices to stick a strip of tape which, straddling an edge, unites the edges on either side. To perform this office correctly, the tape must present a fold: this is the third twist (–). One verifies that one has indeed constructed a Möbius strip, by making a two‑turn cut in the obtained object. What falls out is indeed a bilateral strip and a Möbius strip, more easily recognisable as such, since it has acquired by this operation the dissymmetry of edges that we usually know it to have.

One of the arguments of the supporters of the single‑twist Möbius strip is that the gluing twists cancel each other out once the gluing is done. Yet, here, it is clear that no cancellation is possible, highlighting the same fact when the dimensions present a difference in length.

![](images/9a409fe9b9661dd95f4558b71ccdc04950e12170c8a088e5209a3b33dc808f1e.jpg)

Having analysed the mode of construction of his object, the subject can once again throw it in the bin, with his sophisticated square.

## Demonstration 4: from analytic geometry to topology

Let us write on our paper (in this case, a computer screen – so let us specify: let us write on the two dimensions, the necessary support of writing) a strip of paper – i.e., the flattening of a very simple strip of paper:

![](images/dd4d7a71a6217de321279ff8fa309facb0326151652f1a4fa0c26ccfa3083e47.jpg)

Let us note the dimensions that weave its surface, x and y. Let us note its four corners by letters. Their respective coordinates in the xy reference frame written by the strip itself will be:

```text
- O {x = 0, y = 0}
- A {x = 0, y = a}
- B {x = b, y = 0}
- C {x = b, y = a}
```

Let us construct, from this strip, a Möbius strip. To do this, we must leave the plane of writing, in order to bring C to O and B to A. In what we experience as the reality of the experiment, it seems to us that we are making only one twist, by putting the said writing plane in contact with that which forms its Other side. The latter is not written, and even if we write the same rectangle twice as above, adding in a note: the first is One side, the second is the Other side, we will not have the continuity required by a Möbius strip. For each of these writings, we will suppose a hidden side, which is still not the one written next to it, but which represents it.

By a reversal R performed according to the y‑axis, we only bring C (new reference) to the place of A (old reference) and B (new reference) to the place of O (old reference):

![](images/5ca2e8b5e56e8bb44a30a81a44a2aba000e9f57d06a0e4b80d519cd7d2e74fd2.jpg)

However, while having performed a progressive movement in the direction of x previously established, we have reversed that direction. In the reference frame of the previous writing, x went from left to right; in the new reference frame of the new writing, it goes from right to left. Therefore, while having added, with reference to the old reference frame, a measure b = 2b, we have subtracted a measure –b in a totality that takes into account the doubling of reference frames.

By a second reversal R', according to the x‑axis, we can then bring C to what was O in the first reference frame, and A to what was B in the first reference frame. In so doing, we have reversed the y dimension. While having added a measure a downwards, we have subtracted a measure –a. We have constructed a writing of the Möbius strip:

![](images/83e1d4d2544901b2979a1aa99e26d8eb2fc675bc3c49b60cfad298f8adb6ceca.jpg)

The two dimensions x and y are at once cancelled from a global point of view that takes into account the double change of reference frame, and not cancelled from the local point of view of each new reference frame put in place.

The operations 2a = a – a and 2b = b – b wrote the paradox of a writing that poses the operation without performing it. They tell us moreover that the surface 3S = 3ab is at once a surface – the product of two dimensions (in absolute value) – and a cut (in relative value): the first reversal relatively cancels the x dimension, and theoretically reduces the surface to its y dimension. The second reversal cancels the y dimension with respect to the previous writing, but not the x dimension with respect to the second writing. This time, the surface is theoretically reduced to its x dimension.

Globally, the double movement of passage into the third dimension has cancelled xy – i.e., the surface. We have constructed a hole.

This hole supports the paradox of being nonetheless a surface, since the distinction of times allows one always to preserve a relative dimension (therefore a cut, and not a hole), and, locally, two dimensions in absolute value. According to the point of view, our apparatus therefore presents itself as having zero, one, two or three dimensions.

We have returned to the point of view of the initial One surface. Yet this One is written as a third surface, in another place than the initial surface. We must therefore still identify it with the first One surface, otherwise nothing tells us that this One is the same as the previous One.

We shall perform this:

By a reversal r (a rotation) around point B – the only one that has not changed place since the beginning – we identify the first and third positions of A, O, C. This identification will not have been in vain, since we now have a Controlled Appellation of Origin. By imagining what happens in this rotation from the point of view of the 3rd dimension, we understand that we glue onto the initial green surface the red surface that forms the underside of the green surface we are manipulating. This is where we identify red side and green side, which leads me to colour this identification yellow – "both red and green", hence yellow. However, this localisation is entirely relative. We could colour yellow the second obtained side, a priori red, since by cancelling the x dimension, only the y dimension virtually remains, otherwise writing this paradox of the Möbius strip – which is to be an edge (in principle: one dimension) that nonetheless presents a surface (two dimensions). According to the same reasoning, virtually cancelling the y dimension, the third obtained side, green, could equally well be coloured yellow. Indeed, an identification of the two sides inevitably occurs on the edge, x or y, insofar as it is common to both sides. "Common" implies that it belongs to both sides and that one cannot choose one of the two colours, red or green, requiring the use of a third, yellow.

![](images/7afee36c6a13b5ef8de68058958e547324793589d03be4fbcf41def43bed022c.jpg)

I have several times used the word virtual, because my demonstration works on the displacements of a paper rectangle, each of its positions being only the trace, the memory of its previous position. Each of these movements presents itself as an attempt to return to a supposed original writing – for example the first green one, but it could be any one in this circuit. In other words, each zone, each signified (green) produced by the movement (of speech) remains insufficient to identify with what I suppose to be the signification (red) that I seek to say, without ever succeeding.

We find again the Freudian antinomy of memory (writing, fixed) and consciousness (speech, movement) – a tension that organises the structure of the psychic apparatus.

To write the analysis of the movements necessary for the construction of a Möbius strip, we are constrained to describe three operations: two similar (the two reversals) and one different (the rotation).

They consist of three movements, each around one of the axes of 3‑space:

- the first around the x‑axis
- the second around the y‑axis
- the third around the z‑axis that pierces the writing support at point B.

This we can write in condensed form as follows, which is the correct writing of a Möbius strip:

![](images/29ea682baece472130fcf02b8ab1b73776da8f77ce9566b5b803fb025fc786f9.jpg)

We read there the three sides that were necessary to arrive at this writing: the One side (green), the Other side (red), and the one that imaginarises the edge between the two – the One that is Other (yellow): One because similar to the first, but Other because it is in another place. We assign to each twist the letter designating the operator at work in each of them – i.e., the axial dimension of the movement.

We can also note the similarity of the two twists combining each a dimension of the plane (x or y) and the z dimension of space: +, +, as opposed to the one that reverses the two dimensions of the plane (x and y) without touching z, (–). In other words, the two similar operations have their operators in the plane of writing, while the third takes its operator in the Other dimension – the dimension of the hole that surrounds the writing plane, where I situate speech.

Thus, each of the dimensions, as operator of twist (function), is represented by the surface that faces it (object). Z, in capital letters, is therefore the surface that represents the third dimension separating the One and the Other sides – i.e., the edge.

If the coordinates of the starting points were:

- O {x = 0, y = 0}
- A {x = 0, y = a}
- B {x = b, y = 0}
- C {x = b, y = a}

at the end, we have C at O and B at A. Therefore, we have:

- the point OC: {x = 0, y = 0} and {x = b, y = a} i.e.: {x = b = 0, y = a = 0}
- the point AB: {x = 0, y = a} and {x = b, y = 0} i.e.: {x = b = 0, y = a = 0}

We have theoretically constructed a projective plane, having brought all the coordinates of the object back to the zero of the function.

But we have done so in three times that have allowed us to develop:

- the point of view of the surface as representing the product of two dimensions – the One surface, green, S = ab
- the point of view of the surface as representing a cut – the Other surface, red, of dimensions {y = a, x = b – b = 0}
- the point of view of the surface as representing a hole – the One bis surface, green {x – x = b = 0, y – y = a = 0}.

In which case, we have a model for a theory of representation, which is closer to psychoanalysis than a theory of the object.

This done, we can throw all these writings into the bin, along with the papers that served as support for the previous demonstrations. And click on "empty trash".

## Demonstration 5: manual work – construction of the homogeneous Möbius strip

The single‑twist Möbius strip might find its justification from the three‑twist Möbius strip: if the first has only one twist, it would suffice to add two to obtain the second.

Let us construct a "three‑twist" Möbius strip: we perform the same movements as for the "one‑twist" strip – we are obliged to already make one as demonstrated above, then to make two to glue the two sides.

![](images/d1b990692a2c515907e9d4167d72d1075405d48db12871b566377cec58b85ac6.jpg)

The difference between the heterogeneous strip (where one twist is not in the same direction) and this one, homogeneous (where all twists are in the same direction), does not therefore lie in the number of twists, but in their direction. It is still not possible to construct a single‑twist strip, or even a two‑twist strip. No twist has had to be added compared to the previous exercise. One verifies: by a simple finger movement that reverses the direction of the twist, one passes from the so‑called "one‑twist" configuration to the so‑called "three‑twist" configuration. In other words, the so‑called "three‑twist" could just as legitimately be entitled "one‑twist". But one clearly sees that the question is not there.

Flattening confirms the three twists, and to the homogeneity of the direction of the twists is added the evidence of the homogeneity of the sides: all the zones are at once above the one that follows and below the one that precedes. It is not possible, as in the heterogeneous strip, to colour one side green (above) and one side red (below). Everything must be coloured yellow, like the single above‑below zone of the heterogeneous strip.

![](images/209c1b2fb7f93902c8e9e15cf99fe42e37f1e4b8f622ac4363699d3c068547e9.jpg)

This justifies the interest of these writings as opposed to the drawing:

![](images/a6b004c2539163300e32ab3b79b2a0d91eed0571537cacca6336ca3bf467106e.jpg)

...which does not allow the reading of the two opposite‑direction twists.

On the other hand, this allows a better understanding of the argument of those who insist on considering the Möbius strip as a single‑twist object: what makes the twist, finally, is the difference between the directions of the twists; whence one could say the twist is the one noted "< – >" ... on condition of adding that, then, the strip they call "three‑twist" in fact has none, since the difference is erased. Dialectical return: if it has none, and knowing that all the sides isolated by the writing are discordantial, then it is the twist as such. Which amounts to confirming my initial proposition: the twist, if there is only one, can only be triple.

New dialectical return: the homo strip, if it is the twist as function, does not function, for it produces no object. Its implementation (i.e., its writing) does not allow passage from One side to an Other side, since there is no locality. That is to say: the writing does not allow one to isolate a locality; of course, one can always say that at a point of the object there is a locality. But this "there is" is an implicit writing, which, while referring to the object in an unavowed ontology, writes without saying the difference of the two sides that can indeed be read on the writing of the hetero strip (I say "there is" because I read these two sides, and if I read them, it is because I have written them[^8]). One cannot perform this reading on the writing of the homo strip. Its writing isolates only surfaces which, being all at once above and below, are not distinguished from one another, nor are they distinguished from an edge: an edge is what is at once above and below. The homogeneous Möbius strip represents the function frozen as object, as it presents itself in psychosis. For it to function, it would have to prove it by producing objects that would differentiate themselves from the function: this is what happens in the writing of the hetero strip.

Conclusion: one cannot do without the three twists.

## Demonstration 6: writing is a cut

I call "drawing" the inscription that allows only one twist to be read, because it does not analyse the structure of the Möbius strip. However, as I presented it above, a drawing artefact allows one to present this structure by colouring the edges. In the reality of the drawing, no accident of line, no difference allows one to prejudge this structure:

![](images/02bd520cb8ef3db80e0af7aa29d055bc63c65a8bcfec504788e024e507729d47.jpg)

The fact of writing it thus:

![](images/0b6192b311685aa6856af2074fc559bbb44c7c2d4d68e92cc72bafb82b8da3f6.jpg)

...indicates the inscription as an artefact of a knowledge obtained from experience. What experience? That of the longitudinal cut.

Through the middle, it re‑crosses after one turn and allows one to obtain a bilateral strip with homogeneous twists:

![](images/283be62347de0ebfa5b52916b6d526e4960dbc58510adbc3bd04a4283126f050.jpg)

This makes this strip spherical from the global point of view (two sides), but aspherical from the local point of view: each zone is at once above and below with respect to local reference frames (the previous zone, the following zone). It is also spherical from the global point of view of surfaces (heterogeneous) but aspherical from the point of view of the twists (homogeneous). To obtain this knowledge, one must not only perform this cut, but also make the experience of writing. It is only by colouring (or by making some mark all along one side) that one realises this: the squiggles obtained after cutting do not speak; only this writing "makes it speak" by revealing this structure, which is henceforth inseparable from writing, from the real of writing.

Along the edges, the cut requires two turns to re‑cross, and produces the same object as above, but interlaced with a remainder exactly similar to the starting strip.

![](images/4aa5fe83841839ab00daf2340c7c943c3c45b967f35f5f5b4f2a7f3d74e2b931.jpg)

As previously, one only obtains this knowledge through the experience of writing on the two objects. In other words:

**Act**: cut with ONE turn → **object** → **ONE writing** on the object → **knowledge**: the obtained object is bilateral.

**Act**: cut with TWO turns → **TWO objects** → **TWO writings**, one on each object → **knowledge**: one is bilateral, the other is unilateral.

There is therefore a congruence between the number of cuts, the number of objects, and the number of writings; and if there had been no writing, one would not have known how to qualify or differentiate the objects: there is no object except through writing. The bilateral only takes on meaning in relation to the unilateral, and vice versa. A twist only takes on meaning in relation to another. A zone is only bilateral or unilateral in relation to the previous zone, the next zone, or the set of zones... etc. What is written – knowledge – is always only the difference engendered by the fact of writing.

Now the flattening of a strip, whatever it may be, is equivalent to a writing because it allows one to read:

![](images/067150fea06f4d3ecebf1ca23491e17898cbdc5fa05816195b2b4ffc39cda739.jpg)  
Flattening = cut = writing

One reads very well, in fact, that there is an above surface (green), a below surface (red), and an above‑below surface (yellow): flattening has therefore produced the same thing as a two‑turn cut. It reveals here that it is a question of the structure of the Möbius strip – not a structure "in itself", for nothing is ever "in itself", but always "for someone" – i.e., for a certain point of view. The produced object – the bilateral red and green – remains interlaced with the unilateral yellow, just as, in the writing above, the zones are linked to each other by twists. The twists are therefore also the writing of the cut.

It is not possible to write a Möbius strip "in itself", like a purely transposed object. I have coloured the writing on the right in yellow to express the primary disorientation, but that is the writing artefact, veiling in yellow what the writing nonetheless writes. Every drawing turns out to be the product of a point of view, and if this one proposes the point of view of a knowledge transposed into the drawing:

![](images/dc79ff2c096236694dbf9d06841ecf213859b83eccd840de2afd56b5aa7077a5.jpg)

![](images/1bfe7a95cc4ecea9a20fc8f7f01ef0dd24728685b10e5f37a04301920bc98ba0.jpg)  
Flattening = cut = writing

...not knowledge about "the object" but about a subject developing the various possible points of view on an object through the accidents of writing as such – i.e., of what is identifiable as difference within the very real of writing.

Thus it is with the flattening on the couch, which allows the subject to develop the different points of view on his fantasy object; this has been called "the traversal of the fantasy". He will be able to read live the writings he produces at the very moment of their production: slips during the session, dream narratives, parapraxes and symptoms. It is not a matter of recovering the real of an origin and an "objective" history; it is rather a matter of reconstructing it by writing it. This will allow a precise writing to be established – i.e., oriented (green side, red side) – and will always leave aside a part of the unorientable (yellow: impossible to decide whether above or below), cause of desire: the *objet a*. And it is this that will allow new orientations – i.e., the setting back into movement.

## Interest of the three twists for psychoanalysis

The interest of the distinction – homogeneous Möbius strip, heterogeneous Möbius strip – lies in a reference to the grammar of Damourette and Pichon, which Lacan made use of as is known. The heterogeneous writes the discordantial negation – the "it is not my mother" I mentioned in Demonstration 1. It is also the "I fear that he may come" where the "may" – called "expletive" by Lacan – represents the subject of enunciation in the hesitation of his desire: on one side I fear, on the Other side I desire... what? that he may come – the object cause of desire, represented on this third side which is neither on one side nor the Other, but inclines the subject to lean to One side or the Other. The "may" is represented by the twist between an "I fear" and an "I desire" – the ambivalence of feelings, where One can tip over into the Other thanks to that opening maintained by the object (surface) by virtue of its distinction from the subject (twist = edge ≠ surface). The object is like the stick that keeps the crocodile's mouth open. The counterpart of its existence is the functionality of the function Φ, which maintains the possibility of twist – i.e., the fabrication of object by the passage from one side to the other.

> "It is the ἕτερος, let us note, which, by appearing to mix with discord, erects man in his status – which is that of hommosexual. Not of my office, I emphasise, but of Freud's, who returns this appendage to him, and in full letters." (Lacan, *L'Étourdit*, p. 24)

This heterogeneity of subject and object (witness that the function functions, by producing object) does not appear on the homogeneous Möbius strip. In the writing of the latter, it is impossible to distinguish a locality. In the writing of the heterogeneous, locality is inscribed from the signs of perception (cf. the *Wahrnehmungzeichen* of Letter 52): between + and – it is not the same place as between + and +, nor as between – and +. But between + and + there is the same difference as between a gendarme[^9].

As a result, it is impossible to distinguish the surface from the edge. As a result, the surface is only edge, or the edge is only surface: if the surface is thing‑representation, and the edge word‑representation, then words there are equivalent to things: we have a good writing of schizophrenia. The function no longer engenders an object: it is itself blocked as object. The twisting movement indeed finds a way to be written, but precisely, it never ceases to be written. No opposite‑direction twist comes to bring it a limit.

![](images/de2fc292e23bfc57091b1b595344ad58a94c6cae4b39b16e22d1ad497769d95e.jpg)

## A writing of the unconscious

If we take up the writing of the schema of Freud's Letter 52 to Fliess:

...we can transcribe its data onto the heterogeneous Möbius strip, following Freud's indications, which consider that the two extremities must meet:

![](images/9ccab3fa482550d2aacbce6968f19c3cbfccc5f1c8da551aa6fda09f503c0cbe.jpg)

Following the indications of the "Metapsychology" (1915), we proceed to a triple encoding, as Freud proposes from Letter 52:

**Signs of perception** will be the differences perceived in the direction of the folds. Starting from this minimal difference, dimension is created, in accordance with Poincaré's definition[^10].

![](images/fd2dcce26a479345babfb9c919f8daddeee5a97a7a3b3f7da6cec2470976a0c9.jpg)

**Thing‑representations** – the domain of the unconscious alone – will be the surfaces. A 2nd dimension comes to articulate itself with the first: the product of two dimensions is indeed a surface. As Freud wrote in the "Project", consciousness is incompatible with memory. The former is an effect of speech, the latter an effect of its recording – hence of its writing. The linearity of speech is therefore twisted by what is in memory (written), and by what is in the process of being inscribed in memory by virtue of its current effect during enunciation.

**Word‑representations**, situated in the preconscious, will be the edges. To the hole‑making of enunciation, they oppose the linearity of discourse, which, articulating signifiers in a chain, closes in a loop to produce signified (surface). Word‑representations therefore frame surface, transforming it into a figure one can grasp – i.e., into a writing, to which one gives the name signified.

The edge represents the writing of the 3rd dimension, absent as such from writing.

There is no signified except from the effect of the signifier, and the latter is inscribed in this form of the edge, which represents what, in writing, is not there – movement (more precisely represented by the fold) – and the third dimension, which, by its absence, poses itself as metaphor of the loss of the voice.

![](images/39021a65682e40a6093296b6da44dc2a03f4c523ab5cb0dce614566584083baf.jpg)

What cannot be said never ceases not to be written, until a writing allows something to be said about it – but not everything. What has not found speech is written in one of the formations of the unconscious, notably in the dream, where it is clear that the possibility of speech is cut off, as is all access to motility. The paradox of the Möbius strip well represents the paradox of the psychic apparatus: there is only one side (∀x Φx), but writing imposes a local denial, where there are two (∃x ¬Φx); its single edge (¬∃x ¬Φx) is the trace of a cut that has produced local orientation, but not everywhere (¬∀x Φx).

![](images/e21e7bb4754dc3fa54ad055765bca89d331f053e51a56eab489839b3227ad9d9.jpg)

The interest of the Möbius strip written with its three heterogeneous twists lies in this: that the framing of thing‑representations by word‑representations produces two kinds of inscriptions, two kinds of surface: oriented surfaces (One above, the Other below) and the disoriented surface (the one that is above‑below). Its paradox lies in the fact that this apparently global framing leaves outside the frame the totality of the strip, represented in the writing by the above‑below side.

The writing on the right combines these data with some of those advanced by Lacan in the graph of "Subversion of the Subject and Dialectic of Desire". I have allowed myself to reverse the direction of the upper arrow, so as to make this writing of the graph more conformable to this writing of the Möbius strip. From this perspective, and if Lacan's two parallel arrows indeed represent the Freudian double inscription – thing‑representation, word‑representation – the upper arrow, thing‑representation, should in fact be written by surface, or else, not parallel but perpendicular to the first, so as to produce surface. But this is what can already be read in Lacan's writing: no surface without edge, and it is between these two arrows as edges that he indeed writes the fantasy, \$ ◇ a, if one is willing not to be fooled by its punctual localisation. This localisation responds to the opening of desire, d, placed opposite – of which one recalls that, in his construction of the graph, Lacan placed it at the point where the ascending arrow divides, inaugurating between its branches... surface. In this case, a hole occupying a certain surface that the fantasy covers.

![](images/3c63ebd25adeb84fb1353a57e8db8c652df384963e2e28ad6a29b86c9ae6fc44.jpg)

One must grasp that these concepts are not localised at a point in the schema. Freud, in Letter 52, inscribes the five instances in the same way, by small crosses designating points. Lacan, in his graph, does not distinguish between surfaces on the one hand, and paths and crossing points on the other. Nor does he differentiate between surface and hole. For example, A is indicated as a crossing point. One could attribute to it a surface, pierced by S(barred A), also indicated as a crossing point, but which would better be written, conceptually, as a hole.

One would thus read the Möbius strip as surface A twisted by the hole S(barred A). Or again: thing‑representations being surface, they are everywhere and not localised at a point of the writing; they are the surface as such. Similarly, word‑representations are everywhere, and yet, in the disoriented side, they are as if dissociated from the surface: they do not form an edge in this localisation, because the surface itself being disoriented – at once above and below – it is itself an edge. The edge is what is at once above and below.

One could push further the study of this correspondence between the graph and the topological writings; I propose to do so later.

The formula "point‑by‑point correspondence" is evidently not valid here, since one moves from points and lines to edges, surfaces and holes. No doubt this is true of any translation, and *a fortiori* of the translation of the formations of the unconscious, to which such exercises prepare.

## A writing of neurosis

Let us make a traversal of the heterogeneous Möbius strip, noting the folds by distinctive signs according to their direction. + for the passage from above to below, – for the passage from below to above. Our reference will be the extrinsic reading we logically make of the writing, as it presents itself to us. We do not suppose a hidden side on the other side of the page, since the page is unilateral: we never read recto and verso at the same time. Moreover, the Other side is precisely already there in this writing, in another location, on the same side that presents itself to the reader; one only has to read it. Writing has replaced above‑below by "next to". But we can attempt to confront the extrinsic of the real of our reading with the intrinsic of an imaginary traversal of the surface: this could be a good model for the encounter of the Subject and the Other.

Let us therefore traverse this writing starting from the top, on this part of the yellow zone where I have written "above".

![](images/63a321bca0649abb96a617062fbc06c9c615af62763385549ddd58e76a4c5181.jpg)

Our traversal as readers encounters no difficulty, until we find ourselves on the starting yellow zone: "before", we had read it "above"; "now", the logic of our traversal has brought us to a "below"...

Let us note this well: whatever the starting point you choose, the result is the same. It is pure logic: we have a writing surface – the Möbius strip – which has three zones. And, to orient ourselves, we want to inscribe on these 3 zones a differentiation with two elements – above and below. This is a confrontation of 3 and 2 – of odd and even. Inevitably, if you successively write "above" then "below" at each crossing of a threshold, after three changes you fall on a contradiction: what you had noted "above" at the start finds itself with the mention "below" resulting from the logic "of 2 on 3" of the traversal. Even if you started from the green surface: after one turn, if you noted it "above" at the start, it finds itself "below" at the end. This is also true for the red side, and for the yellow side.

This is the structure of the Möbius strip: it is everywhere at once "above" and "below". What I inscribe there with an "above" green side, a "below" red side, and an "above‑below" yellow side is the writing of a singular traversal, corresponding to what is written of the strip by its flattening. This simple traversal suffices to reveal the structure of the strip: locally there is an above and a below, but globally everything is above‑below, which is locally inscribed in the yellow zone, by virtue of this real of writing that is flattening. From this homogeneity, the heterogeneous Möbius strip nonetheless allows a heterogeneous writing in three distinct zones, which the homo strip does not permit. The function functions all the more in that it can be written (yellow zone), and that next to it one can write the object it produces (red‑green). This homogeneity of the hetero strip is of the cut in act. In the homo strip, it is of the object.

One must pay close attention to this paradox: the yellow zone locally writes what the global structure of the Möbius strip is.

This yellow side is therefore at once above and below... We are confronted with a discordantial negation: I fear that it may be below, where we might be tempted to hear only a forclusive negation: what is above is not below.

"I" – the "I" that desires – can at that moment fear the contradiction, like little Hans who is afraid of wishing ill of his father because he is a rival for his mother, while at the same time he loves him. "I" recoils before the logical contradiction, and, in inhibition, refuses to go further. "I" stays at home, nailed by phobia.

"I" can also choose regression on my path. I then arrive at the fold I have just passed: I had noted it "–" as passage from below to above. But my path now leads me, if I want to remain logical, to note it "+" since my regression is made in the direction of above to below. I am therefore stuck between the contradictions of my own notation, and from then on, endlessly sent back between an incompatible "u" and "o" and an equally incompatible "+" and "–". This would be a model of obsession.

Finally, I can decide not to see the contradiction of the fold notations, and return all the way to the starting fold. This great regression does not prevent me from falling again, on the other side, on the contradiction of the sides, which stops me again. I remain blocked at that place, which, for want of another nomination, turns out to be the place of hysteria – the place where the twist oscillates between somatic symptom and anxiety, both being surfaces, thing‑representations: the first is an oriented surface, for the symptom, sheltered by the somatic alibi, writes some quite readable message of the unconscious; the second is a disoriented surface between above and below, momentarily illegible, but where the object of anxiety turns out to be the same as the object cause of desire.

Between these two sides, the horizontal side that supports the paths of obsession and those dictated by the inhibition of phobia will take the name of inhibition.

![](images/bc7cf2f25ad2c404078fb98357fa2543e4127655517d599244b5a80b8d1a263f.jpg)  
One sees that it is very easy to pass from one to the other, according to the choices of path.

## From the letter to the signifier: a writing of analysis

Finally, this forclusive dimension represents any symptom or manifestation of the unconscious – a letter that writes what cannot be said. Without the need for symptom, inhibition, or anxiety, a dream, a slip, a parapraxis sometimes suffices to write the contradiction, whether it is situated in the folds, in the sides, or in both, if the narrative of this formation of the unconscious finds an interlocutor. This means: go down there, onto this "below" side which is also "above" – "put both feet in the gutter", as Jean‑Michel Vappereau says, borrowing from Wittgenstein.

I write the differences of side by letters: "u" for above and "o" for below. And on this problematic side – at once above and below, a contradiction due only to my reading and to my own notations inscribed beforehand – I can write side by side "u" and "o", but not on the same edge: i.e., make the narrative of this formation of the unconscious.

This is to launch into a second traversal of the Möbius strip. In other words, if the first traversal represented the one‑turn cut (which produces only a bilateral object – a two‑sided object, forclusive), it is a question of launching into the two‑turn cut (which will produce, in addition to a bilateral object, another interlaced Möbius strip – unilateral, discordantial).

![](images/ce7da9cd5acde07d56f8b3c77e0bae2ef67b869f316051e97636addea51fc3e8.jpg)

I have passed from an encoding of sides and folds to a numbering of edges. I was orienting myself thanks to the signs of perception "+" and "–", which allowed me to read above and below – One side and the Other side. I have now just performed the two‑turn cut – i.e., replaced the thing‑representations "One side", "the Other side" by word‑representations: "o" and "u" – in other words, phonemes. I have found someone to talk to about the formations of the unconscious. I realise that the disoriented object – yellow – which falls from the cut, has exactly the same structure not only as the starting object, but also of the function put into play during the cut:

![](images/0724d0d8673bfccd1a0f80f268d19cbcdc8ce1a33bd8122c3ea816175de48a32.jpg)

And since I have established the equivalence of cut and writing, I can continue in this direction by finding a writing of the function – a writing of speech – on the edges. The Saussurian linearity of the signifier is then inscribed in the blue of the cut. In reading this blue line as it is written – i.e., respecting its cuts – we find a first continuity, the pronounced sound "u" of "above", which, after a turn three times twisted in the unconscious, finds itself heard as the "o" of "below". The same line turns out to be enunciated in two different ways, and yet it is the same enunciation – or better: these are the two edges of the same discordantial enunciation:

![](images/464d789bf1a6b115fb08f02c8bba7c09a036a451bec8a17badd65fc81a400a3d.jpg)

We then distinguish the two lines, forclusive: "u" on one side, "o" on the other. The discordantial‑forclusive opposition of the surfaces (yellow – green/red), i.e., of the letters, is dialectised by the same opposition in the domain of the signifier: u‑o discordantial, u and o forclusive.

Like Emma (cf. the "Project"), I realise that the trauma of the contradiction between the reading "above" and the reading "below" was only due to my own inscriptions. My 2nd passage over the "Möbian" side came into contradiction with the first because of the notation I had left there – because of its writing. Speech allows me to relativise this state of "things", by allowing me to accept the coexistence of a present inscription with a contradictory past inscription – an "o" next to a "u". The putting back into act of the temporality of speech allows another inscription of temporality. What the letter did not allow, the signifier authorises.

The contradiction thus written can be situated in the logical field (+ and –, love and hate, black and white, etc.), in the grammatical field (active or passive, forclusive or discordantial, subject or object, etc.), or in the field of homophony (the same signifier referring to several signifieds).

Everything happens as if I had added an extra twist in place of the yellow side that was problematic because of its disorientation. The passage onto the edges represents the twist that faces it in the writing:

![](images/450635993ac36f4ef0359cb72d359b9a475c0f3bf53bfa12a7efe11813ef88e2.jpg)

It is indeed a passage from above to below, but on the same side – i.e., on the linearity of speech. In my traversal, I can now choose such or such orientation. For a new departure in life (i.e., from this side that was problematic because of the Oedipal contradiction – loving and hating papa at the same time), I can:

- Choose to remain below, because this edge I say "o", and then I can mark out a new traversal, being, on each side, on the Other edge than during my previous traversal. I thus complete the two‑turn cut, and I observe, from my extrinsic position that I have just dialectised from an intrinsic traversal, that all the "o" are "external" and all the "u" are "internal". I have therefore found a writing of above‑below by inside‑outside.

![](images/af79ed76c371add2712d4faf0b02188ec5294caa03a34b077f3c99b6fd0d7f34.jpg)

The correspondence of one with the other is just as arbitrary as the choice I made at the start to situate myself on a so‑called "above" side; I could, choosing the same side, have titled it "below". Each edge can therefore be read as a twist: it indeed operates the passage from above to below. In the centre, as a remainder, one reads in yellow the Möbius strip that has operated the cut – i.e., the passage to the edges – i.e., again, the passage to word‑representations. On it, no letter is inscribed, since it is function, movement – a passage from the letter to the signifier.

- Choose to change edge in order to catch up with the path of my first traversal; from "below" I pass back to "above" by the mere fact of passing from bottom to top of the side.

![](images/00dbd3fdbe65206a62b4da6900500b099cb9189c46aac159bab5ed8225e8f876.jpg)

![](images/db6418475ba13f0eb859d57cf920a6b7280b936c0cc37cf10a0ea1d7a6ae47b4.jpg)

This amounts to considering the traversal on this side as a twist. The surface can therefore be read as an edge – i.e., as a twist. I thus observe that it can be the same on each of the sides.

Whatever my choice, I cease to be blocked by the surface (the object) and I put back into play movement – i.e., function – in this form of articulation of phonemes called speech.

## A writing of psychosis

![](images/3d64393c5e3fc06ef7baa758474bbf527896085710c11c4b234c75661a82da0d.jpg)

Regression from the discovery of the unoriented side, added to the disorientation experienced in return concerning the notation of the fold, can have much more serious consequences than neurosis. Another way of getting out of these two contradictions – this time in a radical manner – is to operate a cut in the strip, in order to restore order in the direction of the folds. Since reality (i.e., the notation I had left during a first traversal) does not correspond to what I perceive (the notation contradictory with the first that my logic constrains me to affix in the same place, during a 2nd regressive traversal), I will change what I perceive, and at the level of the problematic side, I operate this cut which will allow me to change the direction of the twist in order to make it conform to the initial direction, without taking account of the regressive aspect of my path. This cut should be called the *acoupure*, since it intervenes to suppress the efficiency of the cut that the heterogeneous Möbius strip as such is – an efficiency localised in its "–" twist. It is effected by a hole P₀ in the Symbolic – i.e., in word‑representations – in other words, in the edge of the strip – and by a hole Φ₀ in the Imaginary – i.e., in thing‑representations – in other words, in the surface. All this is only another presentation of Lacan's Schema I (in "On the Possible Treatment of Psychosis", in *Écrits*).

We now have an explanatory model of hallucination.

It is the little finger movement that changes the direction of the twist at the moment of constructing the Möbius strip, which was the subject of my Demonstration 5.

It restores order in the twists – i.e., in the movement of speech – but it in no way suppresses the contradiction between One side and the Other side. The unconscious is now "laid open" and expresses crudely, in suffering, the Oedipal contradiction and libidinal demands, in the forms of erotomania, jealousy, and persecution.

Freud wrote, in his study on President Schreber, that these three forms of paranoia describe the three ways of negativing the proposition "I love him, a man". Indeed, this proposition is written by the "–" twisting movement of the heterogeneous Möbius strip. The movement – passage into the third dimension as well as into the supplementary dimension of time – describes the emotion, the feeling as it is experienced (Real), not yet as it is imagined (Imaginary), still less as it is said (Symbolic). In our model, the folds, which represent the movement of twist, will therefore represent love, hate and ignorance. The surfaces distinguished in the writing by the folds will represent the Imaginary of the ego and its object. The edges will be the words by which all this is spoken... or not said.

![](images/7d08f6d175d9e07de345c23395298f97564b993e7187778c0e032aa94580e81b.jpg)

In all cases, the *acoupure* makes the blessed ignorance of the neurotic disappear. According to successive displacements, hate or love will take its place. If attention is directed to the left side of the figure, by the reversal of the direction of the twist written on the left, the consequent homogenisation in the folds will bring love to the place of ignorance. If attention is focused on the contrary to the right, it is a reversal of the two folds noted "+" that will occur, in order to homogenise all the folds in "–". Then it is hate that will displace ignorance.

The imaginary swelling of the surface will lead it to self‑cross and finally close into a Boy surface: this is the delusion of grandeur. The weak distinction established between the ego and its object disappears, and the ego presents itself in its solipsistic omnipotence Imaginary. The function no longer produces an object: it has itself become an object.

Let us note in passing that the same swelling of surface in the case of the hetero strip will produce the cross‑cap – i.e., the fantasy:

![](images/58d549e26970183fdcb3cc5b570f858ac00b14f019f461455e64ad3b3ee86628.jpg)

As Freud says, the neurotic preserves a relation to the object in his fantasy. The heterogeneity between off‑line point and line without point is preserved in the cross‑cap, whereas it is annulled in the Boy surface (above left).

> "The remarkable thing about this sequence is that the asphere (written l', apostrophe), starting from the torus (it presents itself there first hand), only comes to the evidence of its asphericity by supplementing itself with a spherical cut." (Lacan, *L'Étourdit*, p. 28)

> "It is the spherical topology of this *objet a* that projects onto the other composite, heterogeneous, that constitutes the cross‑cap." (*L'Étourdit*, p. 30)

A minimum heterogeneity is preserved in the homogeneous Möbius strip: it is the difference between words and things, between edge and surface – in other words, between function and object: the paranoiac speaks, without concern for the contradiction brought to him by reality (i.e., his previous writings). The Boy surface completes the suppression of this last heterogeneity. But this is in an attempt at healing: we saw in Demonstration 4 – which can be done just as well for the homo as for the hetero, simply by changing the direction of one of the reversals – that the final identification of surfaces allows the conjunction of several points of view, including that of a double surface (therefore, in 4 dimensions, projective plane) and that of a hole (zero dimension). The psychotic extension of the surface into the 4 dimensions of the Boy aims to find the path to zero dimension that would set the function back in motion. This is the trajectory of President Schreber and of delusions of grandeur in general: the inflation of the delusion leads to the end of the world (loss of the object) and the death of the subject (loss of the ego), who reads its announcement in the newspaper. On these ruins, it will be possible to engender "a new humanity".

On the contrary, the contraction of the surface until its reduction to the edge will lead to the metonymic discourse of the schizophrenic. He considers words as things, because the only things at his disposal are words.

> "Here appears the decisive difference between the dream‑work and schizophrenia. In the latter, it is the words themselves, in which the preconscious thought was expressed, that become the object of the primary process; in the dream, it is not the words, but the thing‑representations to which the words have been reduced. The dream knows a topical regression, which is not the case in schizophrenia. The dream interpretations we make in analytic practice precisely weaken our feeling of this difference. When the interpretation of the dream follows in trace the course of the dream‑work, follows the paths that lead from latent thoughts to the elements of the dream, discovers the use made of the double meaning of words, and indicates the verbal bridges that connect the different circles of material (*Materialkreisen*), it produces an impression that is sometimes that of wit, sometimes that of schizophrenia, and makes us forget that all operations bearing on words are for the dream only a preparation for regression to thing‑representations (*Sachregression*)." (Freud, "Metapsychological Supplement to the Theory of Dreams", GW X, p. 418, Gallimard, p. 134)

![](images/7c0d8b737e31d3e99352bb5368aa1b5424afc72e0286e2f74b43da2307a786b2.jpg)

![](images/ed4ad2d77ac022801bc21df099ad910e7d82d00ff7732631c56cbf5661cc2485.jpg)

The interpretation of the dream will therefore be the reduction of the hetero strip to its edge – the inner figure‑of‑eight (above right) – while schizophrenia will be the same reduction applied to the homo strip (above left).

On the homogeneous strip, the forclusive negation is generalised to the whole structure. It is not put in relation with the discordantial negation. This is why one can call it foreclosure.

![](images/51ca389e61e2c3df71a8788199f55550fbbf294cb5e03b96ab630c9a8fdfe375.jpg)

One will note however that it is possible to perform exactly the same experience of a double traversal on the homo strip and on the hetero strip, by naming the sides by phonemes. Speech is also the said‑solution of psychosis... if this model holds up.

But in this domain, the study is far from finished, since one knows that the two‑turn cut in the homo strip indeed gives a bilateral interlaced with another unilateral... but this interlacing is triple, and the unilateral obtained is another homo strip. One only returns to the hetero strip if one has recourse to a new cut, similar to that made to change the direction of the twist.

*Monday, 28 October 2002*
