# Dream and Mathematics

I had promised an articulation with practice of my essay on a mathematical representation of the psychic apparatus, developed here:  
<http://une-psychanalyse.com/calcul_integral_et_differentiel.pdf>.

Here is that articulation, based on a recent dream:

I am on a road, looking for my way. I arrive at a crossroads; there are signposts, but not all of them. None points in the direction I am interested in. Further on, another road, another crossroads, where perhaps there will be more signposts.

So I go to the other crossroads, I choose my road.

I realise I have made a mistake. I should have… it’s a matter of medication, even an injection… an injection for someone. It’s very urgent. I have to go back; so I immediately leave the road to turn back. I pass through a very deep V‑shaped valley, and I don’t know how I manage it, I cross over a viaduct that spans this very deep valley.

I arrive at work, where I am delivering. I am a postman, Chronopost or something like that. I am with a colleague (male or female) and we finished very early, around 11:30. I have another job. I think it’s the hospital one; I could go there straight away but it’s not yet time to start. My colleague tells me she has something else for me to do, but she says there isn’t enough time. Perhaps I should hide while I am at loose ends. But I answer that I don’t have to hide: if I’ve finished my job, then I’ve finished my job, that’s that. If it had been on my own initiative, I would have gone to my other job right away.

She takes me to the canteen. There is a kind of chain going up with metal panels; the food goes up inside it, in small metal baskets, and at the same time it serves as a lift for us. People get on it; there are a lot of people, it looks like a university restaurant.

I’m not hungry at all, because 11:30 is too early.

At the outset, I immediately recognise the Real, that place where I am fatally lost since there are no signposts, that is to say no representation. That is why I have to go back, towards better‑signposted places. As usual, it is on the edge of the Real, on my way back from it, that I find sex. The V‑shaped valley is a female sex. So the injection must be a penetration. That is it, the medication of castration. It is filling the hole with the phallus, here represented by the syringe. The viaduct is another method: passing over it is a way of skipping over it.

In reality, I was worried about receiving my passport, which a visa company was supposed to send back to me after having it stamped at the Chinese embassy. They were to send it to me by Chronopost. Yet I had phoned the caretaker of my building two days before: no problem, my Chronopost package had arrived. I suppose that is not enough for my unconscious, which decides that I am the Chronopost delivery man: it’s safer, even though the conscious mind knows perfectly well that there is nothing to worry about. It is a way of managing a lack where there is none.

I finished my job at the time when I usually start my real work at the clinic. Here, it is the intervention of the superego, which watches over me even while I am asleep. It takes over from the bosses to check that I am doing my job properly and that I am not idle. At the clinic, I sometimes feel guilty when a patient does not show up for their appointment several times in a row and I have not been offered other patients. There is nothing guilty about it, since I do my job and I can’t help it if a client doesn’t come, and I can’t help it either if I don’t find a new patient’s file in my pigeonhole at the secretariat. That’s the superego: a part of myself that, from the inside, takes over from external surveillance, which itself succeeded the parents.

This is what cuts the psychic apparatus in two between conscious and unconscious: I am fully conscious of these worries about the visa for China or my work at the clinic. And sleep does not free me from them. Now, any worry, by similarity, calls upon the fundamental worry, the anxiety of castration, which stands on the edge of the Real where one is lost. On the other hand, those anxieties are entirely unconscious.

For the subject, the right way to get out of it consists in imagining oneself to be responsible for what happens to oneself, rather than suffering an external destiny. Being the driver, or the Chronopost delivery man, is a way of bringing the outside into the inside, in other words, of transforming reality into representations. That is what the child does with the *fort‑da* game: by throwing away an object that represents his mother, he obtains the satisfaction of having himself provoked her departure. Thus, for want of keeping his love‑object, he brings himself into the world as a subject by throwing it away on his own initiative. I think everyone knows that in love stories: when you feel things are going wrong, you leave first in order to avoid being left. Even if it is imaginary, the gesture performed on one’s own initiative – the expression appears in this dream – symbolises what one cannot master in reality. I am the driver of the cut [coupure] which, by crossing itself [se recoupant], releases a washer in the third dimension of reality. That is where we can understand this necessity of the *acoupure* [pre‑cut] that crosses itself to become the cut: it is the law applying to itself. The cut – that is, the surveillance that comes from outside – I now apply to myself. I gain freedom in external space, insofar as it is I who decide to apply the law to myself. I become myself, that is to say capable of calculating the surface of my ego… by a limitation of the surface of my ego, that is, an imitation of my freedom. That is the question of integral calculus that I had brought up in my reference article.  
<http://une-psychanalyse.com/calcul_integral_et_differentiel.pdf>

This is exactly what I had formulated concerning the necessity of cutting the subject‑function in two in order to calculate, by integration, the surface of the washer of the ego.

For the Real, that doesn’t work. Even at the other crossroads, there are missing signposts, that is to say representations. For reality, it works, but the anxiety of being deprived of my passport has drawn with it, on the one hand, the anxiety of castration (no medication, no injection to fill the female sex, to get over that valley) – the primitive anxiety of being deprived of any means of entering into contact with the other in reality. But in the end, the signposts at the other crossroads are there: one simply has to decode the metaphor. What allows one to orient oneself is precisely the medication (the injection) that repairs this lack, which is clearly marked out by castration (the V‑shaped valley), and which comes as a substitute for the missing representations at the crossroads. The dream is quite precise about that: I forgot the medication, the injection. In other words: I repressed them. On the contrary, the missing signposts are not repressed and they have no reason to be: they simply are not there. That corresponds to what Freud rather poorly called primal repression, for there is no repression. It is in order to find such signposts that I suddenly remember my mistake – that is to say my forgetting. But those ones evoke castration, which is why they appear only in the veiled form of a metaphor. Nevertheless, the injection makes it possible to make a hole, thus avoiding having to suffer it as such. It comes to say: no, originally there is no hole; it is I who am going to make it, at the very same moment when I fill it. Likewise, the bridge makes it possible to cross the abyss.

The difference between drive and desire manifests itself here quite clearly. The drive pushes me towards the territory of the Real where I look for signposts that do not exist. So I turn back to follow the path of desire, which knows what it aims at – the lost phallus – even if it is in coded form. In both cases we see the derivative of the function being written, since the drive drifts between signposts that tell me nothing (they don’t interest me) and signposts that don’t exist, while desire drifts from disavowal (there is no hole except the one I myself create with the syringe) to the indispensable solution (if there is a hole, then there must be a phallic bridge to cross it). That is, in both cases from f(x₀) to f(x₀ + h) when h tends towards 0 – the zero of the absence of representation for the drive, the zero of castration for desire.

And the end of the dream – that strange food lift? Since it carries up both food and humans, I deduce that humans might be food. By regression to the oral stage, eating consists in putting inside what remains outside, the Real. So we are in the presence of the machine for creating representations – that is, an image of the symbolic function. It too stands on the edge of the Real, as does the phallus, which is another image in that it is the instrument of generation. The machine swallows people as well as food, so it can create people as well as things. That inevitably makes me think of the machine that feeds on cats and babies in Janet Cardiff’s work. See: <http://une-psychanalyse.com/inhotim.pdf>.

This machine seems to be part of a university restaurant: that is a way of saying that it is a matter of producing knowledge by swallowing what one does not know. It is, once again, producing representations.
